Laguna Phuket Triathlon · Local economic impact · Written report

What the Laguna Phuket Triathlon weekend brings to the area

The Laguna Phuket Triathlon weekend brings an estimated 35 to 40 million baht a year (about US$1.1 million) into the Laguna and Bang Tao area in a middle case for 2023 to 2025, with a range of about 13 to 110 million baht depending on how long athletes stay and how many people travel with them.

  • The organiser's own projections from about ten years ago, "more than 74 million baht" (around 2016) and "over 90 million baht" (2015 race week), sit inside that range, and the same method gives about 62 million baht when it is run with the 1,100 athletes the organiser assumed.
  • The official results show about 1,060 finishers across every race of the weekend in 2024, the latest year with a list for each race, and 557 finishers in the individual, sprint and duathlon races in 2025, against 700 in 2019.
  • The field has become far more international: athletes recorded as living in Thailand fell from 74% of finishers with a country in 2022 to 19% in 2025, though results before 2024 may have been recorded differently.
  • The race is owned and run by Laguna Resorts & Hotels, which owns four of the six race hotels; the only published impact figures are the organiser's own, no participant survey exists, and no resident complaints were found in the sources that could be read, though several local forums and news pages could not be opened.

The estimate is directional: it is built from public results, list prices and stated assumptions, and each number names its source.

Prepared 9 November 2026 from public sources. Estimate revised 11 November 2026. The 2026 race is on Sunday 15 November 2026 and is not included.

The charts and the adjustable spending calculator are on the dashboard page: Laguna Phuket Triathlon: what the race brings to the area.

About 1,060 people finished a race over the 2024 weekend

The official results for every year from 2017 to 2025 sit on the timing company Sportstats, linked from the organiser's own results page, and every list was pulled in full: rows downloaded matched the site's own total in all 45 lists, and the finisher counts matched the site's "finished" figure on each event page (Laguna Phuket Triathlon results page; 2024 individual leaderboard). A finisher here is anyone with a finish time and no did-not-finish or disqualified flag. These are the exact numbers; everything the press reports is rounded.

The trend is plain: the main 1.8 km swim, 50 km bike, 12 km run race has lost about a third of its finishers since 2019 (537 to 347), and has fallen every year since the 2022 restart (445, 414, 374, 347). The sprint has held steady at around 170 to 210, and the duathlon has halved since 2022. Relay lists show teams, not people, and do not say how many people are in a team (2024 relay list). There was no race in 2021: the 28th edition was announced for November 2021 but held on 20 November 2022 (TTR Weekly, Jan 2021; TTR Weekly, Sep 2022). The 2020 race went ahead under COVID rules with only Thai-coded athletes in the results (Laguna Phuket Tri, 2020).

The event pages also carry a larger "entrants" number that the lists do not explain. For the individual race it was 735 in 2022, 566 in 2023, 474 in 2024 and 436 in 2025, against 491, 565, 395 and 350 rows in the lists. The extra names are probably registered athletes who did not start; that is an interpretation, because the site does not label the number. Even on that wider measure, individual-race entrants fell 41% from 2022 to 2025.

Counting every race of the weekend gives the fuller picture, though lists exist for some years only. In 2024, the latest year with a list for each race, 606 people finished the individual, sprint and duathlon races, 23 relay teams finished (about 69 people at three a team), 283 finished the fun run and 104 finished the open water swims: about 1,060 finishers in all. In 2025 the same count is 741 without the fun run, which has no published list. In 2017 to 2019, when 10 km and 5 km runs and a kids' run were held the day before the race, the weekend had about 1,500 to 2,100 finishers (1,493, 2,082 and 1,695). These are entries, so a person who swam on Saturday and raced on Sunday is counted in both. The organiser's press releases say "more than 1,000 triathletes" in 2018, 2019, 2023, 2024 and 2025, and "more than 1,200 athletes" in 2022 (TTR Weekly 2022; Laguna Phuket 2023; Pattaya Mail 2024; Laguna Phuket Tri 2025). They do not say whether that counts entrants, starters or finishers, or which races, and the weekend totals above are in line with them. The 2025 organiser estimate of "over 3,000 spectators" is a single unverified figure from the organiser (Laguna Phuket Tri, 31st preview).

The 2026 race, the 32nd, is on Sunday 15 November 2026, with an open water swim on Saturday 14 November; online registration closed on 8 November and no entry number had been announced by 9 November (early bird release; individual entry page). No 2026 count or estimate is possible yet.

Athletes recorded as living in Thailand fell from 454 in 2022 to 108 in 2025, while those from other countries rose from 157 to 449. This fits Laguna's own statement that 2024 hotel revenue rose on "more foreign visitors" (LRH 2024 results explanation). The fall was sharpest between 2023 and 2024, from 175 to 64 in the individual race, the year registration was split into "Overseas" and "Thais & Expats" entry pages (Overseas 2024; Thais & Expats 2024), so results before 2024 may have been recorded differently. The estimate below uses the recorded share for each year.

The mix of countries also changed. In 2023 the leading foreign sources in the individual race were Singapore (47) and Hong Kong (37); in 2024 and 2025 they were Great Britain, Australia and France. Across individual, sprint and duathlon in 2025, Great Britain and Thailand each had 108 finishers, followed by Australia 47, France 31, Russia 30, Germany 24, the Netherlands 22, the United States 20, Italy 17 and Malaysia 14. Country counts in the press (30, 48, 51, or "30 and 65" in a single 2024 article) are not consistent enough to use (Pattaya Mail 2024).

Every published impact figure is an organiser projection from 2015 or 2016

No independent study of this race's economic impact was found, in English or Thai. The only numbers come from Laguna Phuket itself and are forward-looking estimates, not measured results. A press release on the organiser's site (titled for the 2017 date announcement, with text that may describe the 2016 edition) says the race "is estimated to generate more than 74 million baht spending in tourism segment for Phuket Province" from "more than 1,100 athletes" and their supporters, and that hotel room and food and beverage revenue for Laguna Phuket's own hotels and businesses is "approximated 9 million baht during the race weekend" (lagunaphukettri.com). In October 2015 Prapa Hemmin, Laguna Phuket's director of sales for MICE and corporate events, said the Laguna Tri-Fest week, which then combined this race with a Challenge Laguna Phuket race, was "projected to generate over 90 million baht", on the basis of thousands of athletes each bringing three to five companions and staying four to five nights (Thaiger, 12 Oct 2015). The two figures cover different events and periods and cannot be read as a trend.

Recent official statements give no numbers. A 2025 booklet welcome, attributed to the Governor of Phuket, says the race "plays an important role in stimulating the local economy" (LPT 2025 booklet), and the 2026 release says it will "significantly boost" high-season tourism (LPT, 8 Apr 2026). These are claims, not measurements.

Figures for the June Laguna Phuket Marathon are often quoted alongside the triathlon and must be kept apart: 215 million baht (2017, an article citing Frost & Sullivan research for visitor numbers) (Paul Poole, Nov 2017), 146.8 million baht (2018, an unnamed "independent study") (endurance.biz), and 291.58 million baht (2019, organiser, seen only in a search summary). The marathon draws more than 7,000 runners (The Nation, 2026), roughly ten times the triathlon's field, so its figures say nothing about the triathlon.

The estimate: about 37 million baht brought to the area in 2025

The estimate counts what athletes and the people with them spend on the race and their stay. Everyone who comes to race is treated as a visitor to the Laguna and Bang Tao area, which is a resort and second-home neighbourhood more than a local residential one. It uses numbers from the research notes and three points from an athlete who knows the race: relay teams are usually three people, an athlete usually brings at least one other person, and most athletes stay in the Laguna hotels or in rented condos and guesthouses nearby. Where no number exists, the input is labelled an assumption, with a reason and a range. It is a gross figure: no multiplier is applied, because the notes contain no sourced multiplier for Phuket. The aim is a directional figure.

Who is counted

The headcount is the individual, sprint and duathlon starters, meaning finishers plus non-finishers whose timing chip was read on course (so they did travel and start), plus relay teams at three people each. The fun run, kids' run and open water swims are part of the same weekend. The people in them are treated as the athletes, family and friends already counted here, so they are not added a second time.

The 2025 middle case, worked in full

In 2025, 562 people started the individual, sprint and duathlon races and 33 relay teams started; at three people a team that is 99 more, so 661 athletes. Of these, 19.4% lived in Thailand, which is 128 athletes, and 533 travelled from abroad. Of the 128, 20% lived on Phuket (26) and 102 came from elsewhere in Thailand. With 1.5 companions each, that is about 1,650 people in all.

Each athlete from abroad, with the people travelling with them, spends: entry fee US$277 × 1.087 = US$301.10, × 32.8619 baht per dollar = 9,895 baht; rooms 5 nights × 1.25 rooms × 5,200 = 32,500 baht; food 2.5 people × 5 days × 1,500 = 18,750 baht; transport 1,400 baht. That is 62,545 baht per athlete from abroad.

Each athlete from elsewhere in Thailand spends: entry fee 5,560 × 1.087 = 6,044 baht; rooms 3 × 1.25 × 5,200 = 19,500 baht; food 2.5 × 3 × 1,000 = 7,500 baht; transport 1,400 + half of 3,600 = 3,200 baht. That is 36,244 baht per domestic athlete. Each athlete living on Phuket spends the entry fee of 6,044 baht plus one day of food for 2.5 people at 1,000 baht, which is 8,544 baht.

The total is 533 × 62,545 = 33,336,485 baht, plus 102 × 36,244 = 3,696,888 baht, plus 26 × 8,544 = 222,144 baht, which is 37,255,517 baht, about 37.3 million baht or US$1.13 million. By line: rooms 19.31 million, companions' food 6.49 million, entry fees 6.05 million, athletes' food and local spending 4.33 million, transport and bike 1.07 million. Those numbers mean about 3,714 room-nights ((533 × 5 + 102 × 3) × 1.25), against about 7,460 room-nights in the six Laguna hotels over five nights (1,492 rooms in the Laguna fact sheet), though not every athlete stays in Laguna.

Laid against the visitor statistics, the middle case is cautious: an athlete from abroad and the people with them spend about 25,000 baht a person, against an average of 37,440 baht per international visitor per visit to Phuket in 2024 (C9 Hotelworks, from Ministry of Tourism and Sports data).

What the estimate does not cover

No multiplier is applied, so the figures are first-round spending only, not the wages and supplier purchases that follow; the notes contain no sourced multiplier for Phuket. November is the start of Phuket's high season, and island-wide hotel occupancy was 69% across 2024 (C9 Hotelworks), so some athlete room-nights may have replaced guests who would have stayed anyway; no November occupancy figure was found, so that share cannot be measured. Some foreign athletes may have been in Phuket on holiday regardless, and the estimate counts their whole stay. There is no survey of this race's participants, so stay length, party size and spending are all borrowed or assumed. Entry fees go to the organiser, which pays the race's costs (a US$20,000 prize purse, timing, medical and safety services) out of them; those costs are not published, and part of the prize money goes to foreign professionals. Some of the bike truck money is spent outside Phuket. On the other side, the estimate leaves out spectators who did not travel with an athlete, registered athletes who paid but did not start (about 143 in 2025 if the site's entrant figure counts them), anyone who came only for the fun run or a swim, entry fees for the fun run and the swims, sponsors' spending, and any training camp spending before the race.

How this compares with the organiser's figures

The organiser's 74 million baht for "more than 1,100 athletes" works out at about 67,000 baht per athlete, companions included. The 2025 middle case here works out at about 56,000 baht per athlete. Run with 1,100 athletes, the middle case gives about 62 million baht, and with four companions per athlete, the midpoint of the organiser's three to five, about 112 million baht. So the two approaches agree once the number of athletes is the same. The 2025 figure is lower mainly because 661 athletes started the events counted, against the 1,100 the organiser used. The 2015 figure of 90 million baht also covered a second race, Challenge Laguna Phuket, and stays of four to five nights for "thousands of athletes" (Thaiger, 2015). No 2015 or 2016 exchange rate is in the notes, so those figures are given in baht only.

Laguna Resorts & Hotels owns four of the six race hotels and the race itself

Laguna Phuket is owned and developed by Laguna Resorts & Hotels PCL (LRH), a subsidiary of Singapore-listed Banyan Tree Holdings (now Banyan Group), which held about 86.28% of it when LRH was delisted from the Stock Exchange of Thailand on 12 September 2026 for failing to restore a 15% public float (Banyan Tree Holdings notice, 1 Sep 2026). Of the six hotels marketed as Laguna Phuket and offered on the athlete code, LRH's own filings count four as group hotels: Angsana, Banyan Tree Phuket, Cassia and HOMM Suites, about 983 rooms (LRH Q2 2026 results; Laguna fact sheet). Dusit Thani Laguna Phuket was sold to Dusit Thani PCL in 2010 (Thaiger, 2010), and SAii Laguna Phuket was sold to Singha Estate in 2018 while Outrigger kept running it (Travel Weekly); its current owner was not checked. Boat Avenue, the villa and condo owners who rent to athletes, and Bang Tao's guesthouses and restaurants are independent of LRH.

For scale, the 2025 middle-case spending of 37.3 million baht equals about 1% of LRH's group hotel revenue that year, and only part of it reaches LRH. The race was created in 1994 by IMG with Laguna to promote the resort (Wikipedia), and the 2025 booklet still lists Laguna as organiser, GAA Events as co-organiser, and Laguna staff as volunteers (LPT 2025 booklet).

Giving back is documented in detail only up to 2019

Charity is channelled through the Laguna Phuket Foundation and its Children First Fund, set up in 2017, which the 2025 booklet says has supplied food every month since then to four orphanages housing more than 200 children (LPT 2025 booklet). The specific amounts found are older: a 1,000,000 baht donation to the Kao Project for 11 hospitals announced at the 2017 race press conference by Banyan Tree founder KP Ho (LPT 2017), more than 250,000 baht raised by the 2018 sprint for the Children First Fund (search summary only) (lagunaphukettri.com), and a 2019 fun run that gave 9 baht per kilometre to the Kao Kon La Kao Foundation (Phuket News, 2019). No donation total for the triathlon was found for 2020 to 2025. The race also brings in local partners: the 2025 booklet credits the Cherngtalay Sub-District Administration Organization for water safety, Phuket Police for road safety, Bangkok Hospital Phuket for medical cover, and a community relations coordinator, but gives no figures for local hiring, vendors or volunteers.

Road closures reach villages outside Laguna; no complaints were found in readable sources

The most detailed closure notice found is for 2023. On race morning, all Laguna roads and Lagoon Road were closed from 06:30 to 11:00, and closures on public roads ran from about 05:30 to 11:00 across Road 4030 to Bandon Market, the Road 402 motorbike lane, Road 3029, the Airport Road (4026), the Old Airport Road (4031), Road 4018, Nai Thon and Layan, and village roads in Prusompan, Pruchampa, Nop Pring, Pak Plee, Koktanod and Ban Sakoo; the notice warned of 30 minutes' extra travel and set a practice-day closure on the Friday from 07:00 to 11:00 (LPT23 road closure flyer). In 2018 the Thaiger reported roads around Laguna blocked "at times" until midday (Thaiger, 18 Nov 2018). So the race affects residents well beyond the Laguna gates for one Sunday morning and one practice morning a year. No notices for 2024, 2025 or 2026 were found.

Searches for complaints from residents, villa owners or Bang Tao and Cherng Talay community groups about the race found none. That is not proof that none exist: The Phuket News, ASEAN NOW (Thaivisa) and Phuket Gazette pages returned blocks or errors, and Facebook groups and Reddit could not be searched. The fair statement is "none found in the sources that could be read".

What could not be found

The gaps matter as much as the numbers. There is no survey of this race's participants, so where athletes live, how long they stay, how many people come with them and what they spend are all assumed. Why the share of athletes recorded as living in Thailand fell so sharply after 2023 is not stated. No independent or post-race measured economic impact exists for the triathlon; the 74 and 90 million baht figures are a decade-old organiser projection. Entry fees for 2022 to 2025, and sprint, swim and fun run fees for any year, were not found. The meaning of the event pages' "entrants" figure, how many fun run and swim entrants also raced the triathlon, and fun run lists for 2020, 2022, 2023 and 2025 are missing. November occupancy and room rates for Phuket, Bang Tao rates outside the Laguna hotels, and any sourced multiplier are missing. Laguna does not publish race costs, its race-week hotel revenue, or occupancy as a result. Charity totals after 2019, local jobs, vendor and volunteer numbers, closure notices after 2023, and any record of resident complaints were not found. The 2026 race had not been run at the research date, and no entry count was published.

Conclusion

The triathlon weekend brings roughly 1,300 to 2,300 people to Laguna in a year like 2025, athletes and the people travelling with them, and an estimated 37 million baht of spending in the middle case, most of it on rooms. The field in the triathlon events has shrunk since 2019, and it has become much more international, which raises spending per athlete. The organiser's own decade-old figures are consistent with this estimate once the number of athletes is matched.

The estimate is directional. Three numbers the organiser already holds would firm it up: entries by country of residence for every race of the weekend, hotel nights booked on the athlete rate, and a count of people who entered more than one race. A short participant survey at registration and a published charity total each year would turn most of the remaining assumptions into facts. For residents, the cost is one Sunday morning and one practice morning of closed roads across Cherng Talay and Thalang each year.
