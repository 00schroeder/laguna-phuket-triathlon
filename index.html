      Relay teams finishing, 2017 to 2025 (teams, counted)

      Teams. A team is usually three people.

      The organiser's press releases say "more than 1,000" athletes most years. Across every race of the weekend, the 2024 lists show about 1,060 finishers, which is in line with that. In 2017 to 2019, when 10 km and 5 km runs were held the day before the race, the weekend had about 1,500 to 2,100 finishers. The triathlon, sprint and duathlon on their own had 557 to 666 finishers a year from 2022 to 2025.

      How this was worked out
      • The official results for 2017 to 2025 are on the timing company Sportstats, linked from the organiser's results page. Every list was pulled in full. The rows downloaded matched the site's own total in all 45 lists.
      • A finisher is anyone with a finish time and no did-not-finish or disqualified flag.
      • The event pages also show a larger "entrants" number that the lists do not explain. For the individual race it was 735 in 2022, 566 in 2023, 474 in 2024 and 436 in 2025. The extra names are probably registered athletes who did not start. That is an interpretation, because the site does not label the number.
      • The press figures do not say whether they count entrants, starters or finishers, or which races. The 2022 figure of "more than 1,200 athletes" is close to the entrant numbers on the event pages. In 2024, the triathletes, the 283 fun run finishers and the 104 swimmers together come to about 1,000.
      • There was no race in 2021. The 2020 race went ahead under COVID rules, with only Thai-coded athletes in the results.
      • Relay lists show teams. Three people per team is used for the weekend count.

      Where athletes come from

      The recorded country of each finisher in the individual, sprint and duathlon races.

      Country means where the athlete lives. The entry form asks for country of residence, so a British athlete living in Bang Tao is recorded under Thailand.

      The share recorded as living in Thailand fell sharply between 2023 and 2024, from 175 to 64 finishers in the individual race. That was the year registration split into "Overseas" and "Thais & Expats". Results before 2024 may have been recorded differently.

        How this was worked out
        • Each result row carries a three-letter country code. Blank means the row has no code.
        • Athletes recorded as living in Thailand fell from 454 in 2022 to 108 in 2025, while those from other countries rose from 157 to 449. This fits the organiser's company saying that 2024 hotel revenue rose on more foreign visitors.
        • The spending estimate below uses the recorded share for each year.
        • In 2023 the leading foreign sources in the individual race were Singapore (47) and Hong Kong (37). In 2024 and 2025 they were Great Britain, Australia and France.
        • Country counts in the press (30, 48, 51, or "30 and 65" in one 2024 article) are not consistent enough to use.

        Estimated spending brought to the area Estimate

        This adds up what athletes and the people with them spend on the race and their stay: entry fees, rooms, food and local transport. Everyone who comes to race is counted as a visitor to the Laguna and Bang Tao area. No survey of this race's participants exists, so several inputs are assumptions. Each one is shown. The figure is directional.

        Spending by year, 2022 to 2025 (million baht, estimate)

        Dot is the middle case. The bar runs from the low case to the high case.

        2025 middle case by spending line (million baht, estimate)

        Rooms are the largest line. Most athletes stay in the Laguna hotels or in rented condos and guesthouses nearby.

        How this was worked out
        • Who is counted. People who started the individual, sprint and duathlon races (736, 670, 640 and 562 in 2022 to 2025), plus relay teams (24, 23, 24 and 33) at three people each. The fun run, kids' run and open water swims are part of the same weekend. The people in them are treated as the athletes, family and friends already counted, so they are not added a second time.
        • Where they live. The recorded country for each year: 74.3%, 48.9%, 21.2% and 19.4% of athletes lived in Thailand in 2022 to 2025. Of those, 35% (low), 20% (middle) or 10% (high) are taken to live on Phuket and sleep at home. That share is an assumption. They are counted for their entry fee and one day of food, with no hotel.
        • Companions. 1 (low), 1.5 (middle) or 2.5 (high) per athlete. This is an assumption. An athlete usually brings at least one other person, and 2.49 was measured at Ironman Mallorca. One room is counted for every two people.
        • Nights. 3, 5 or 7 for athletes from abroad and 2, 3 or 4 for athletes from elsewhere in Thailand. This is an assumption. The shortest stay is the night before the race and race night.
        • The 2025 middle case. 562 starters plus 33 relay teams at 3 people each gives 661 athletes. 19.4% live in Thailand: 128 people. That leaves 533 from abroad. Of the 128, 20% live on Phuket (26) and 102 come from elsewhere in Thailand. With 1.5 companions each, that is about 1,650 people.
        • An athlete from abroad and the people with them spend 62,545 baht: entry fee 9,895, rooms 32,500 (5 nights, 1.25 rooms, 5,200 a night), food 18,750 (2.5 people, 5 days, 1,500 a day) and transport 1,400. An athlete from elsewhere in Thailand spends 36,244 baht. An athlete living on Phuket spends 8,544 baht. The total is 533 x 62,545 plus 102 x 36,244 plus 26 x 8,544, which is 37,255,517 baht.
        • Dollar amounts use yearly average rates from the Federal Reserve (FRED AEXTHUS): 35.0500 (2022), 34.7841 (2023), 35.2845 (2024) and 32.8619 (2025) baht per dollar.
        • Low and high cases stack every low or every high input together. They are outer bounds. Most of the width comes from companions, nights and room rates.
        • A check against visitor statistics. An athlete from abroad and the people with them spend about 25,000 baht a person. The average international visitor to Phuket spent 37,440 baht per visit in 2024 (C9 Hotelworks, from Ministry of Tourism and Sports data). The middle case is on the cautious side.
        • The middle case rises from 2022 to 2024 even though the field shrank. More of the field comes from abroad, and visitors from abroad stay longer and spend more.

        What this estimate leaves out

        • No multiplier is applied. Only first-round spending is counted, not the wages and supplier purchases that follow. No sourced multiplier for Phuket was found.
        • November is the start of Phuket's high season, so some hotel nights may have replaced guests who would have stayed anyway. No November occupancy figure was found.
        • There is no survey of this race's participants. Stay length, party size and spending are borrowed or assumed.
        • Spectators who did not travel with an athlete, athletes who paid but did not start, anyone who came only for the fun run or a swim, sponsors' spending, and training camps before the race are not counted.
        • Entry fees go to the organiser, which pays the race's costs out of them. Those costs are not published.
        • Some foreign athletes may have been in Phuket on holiday regardless, and the estimate counts their whole stay. Some of the bike truck money is spent outside Phuket.

        Figures the organiser has published

        No independent study of this race's economic impact was found, in English or Thai. The published figures below are forward-looking projections, not measured results.

        Run with the organiser's 1,100 athletes, the same method as the estimate above gives about 62 million baht in the middle case, and about 112 million baht with four companions per athlete. The organiser's figures sit inside that span. The 2025 estimate is lower mainly because 661 athletes started the events counted.

        For scale, the 2025 middle-case estimate (37.3 million baht) equals about 1% of the company's hotel revenue that year, and only part of it reaches the company.

        How this was worked out
        • The figures come from the company's audited statements for 2019, 2022 and 2023, 2024 and 2025. The 2024 total includes 517 million baht of insurance income.
        • In 2025, selling homes overtook running hotels for the first time.
        • None of the company's results announcements mention the triathlon.
        • The race was created in 1994 by IMG with Laguna to promote the resort. The 2025 booklet lists Laguna as organiser, GAA Events as co-organiser, and Laguna staff as volunteers.

        Race morning and giving back

        What was published about road closures and charity. Details are from the organiser's own notices and booklet.

        Road closures (2023 notice)

        • Laguna roads and Lagoon Road were closed from 6:30 am to 11:00 am on race morning.
        • Public roads were closed from about 5:30 am to 11:00 am. The listed roads and villages are Road 4030 to Bandon Market, the Road 402 motorbike lane, Road 3029, the Airport Road (4026), the Old Airport Road (4031), Road 4018, Nai Thon and Layan, and village roads in Prusompan, Pruchampa, Nop Pring, Pak Plee, Koktanod and Ban Sakoo.
        • The notice warned of 30 minutes of extra travel.
        • A practice-morning closure was set for the Friday, 7:00 am to 11:00 am.
        • No notices were found for 2024, 2025 or 2026.

        Charity

        • The Children First Fund, set up in 2017, supplies food every month to four orphanages with more than 200 children (2025 booklet).
        • 1,000,000 baht went to the Kao Project in 2017.
        • More than 250,000 baht was raised by the 2018 sprint (seen in a search summary only).
        • No donation totals were found for 2020 to 2025.

        No resident complaints were found in the sources that could be read. Several local news and forum sites could not be opened, so this is not proof that none exist.
